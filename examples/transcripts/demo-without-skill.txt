  Two further papers are often cited in this area but measure ex vivo clonotype
  retention through manufacturing, not persistence in the patient — a different
  claim: Ham et al., BJC 2024 (PMID 38750113, doi 10.1038/s41416-024-02707-6)
  and Shah et al., JITC 2022 (PMID 35110355, PMC8811607, doi
  10.1136/jitc-2021-003082).

  Adjacent, not adoptive therapy: Pai et al., Cancer Cell 2023 (PMID 37001526,
  doi 10.1016/j.ccell.2023.03.009) clonally links exhausted tumour-specific CD8
  cells to TCF7⁺SELL⁺ nodal progenitors and shows persistence in blood years
  after checkpoint blockade — clonal lineage inference, observational. Ishihara
  et al., Oncoimmunology 2026 (PMID 42503644, PMC13418702) found high-avidity
  HPV E6* TCRs persisted in metastasis while low-avidity clones were lost; the
  avidity measurement is functional, the persistence is observed.

  Evidence bearing on causation

  All of it is in mice or in vitro. The designs differ in how much they license
  a causal claim:

  Removal experiments — the strongest form here. Ferry et al., JITC 2026 (PMID
  42236119, PMC13239385, doi 10.1136/jitc-2025-013020): depleting armoured CAR T
  cells in preconditioned mice caused loss of durable tumour control. This
  shows continued presence of the transferred population is required for the
  effect. It does not show that persistence of particular clonotypes is causal —
  the depletion removes the whole population.

  Necessity of a step. Wittling et al., Cancer Immunol Res 2026 (PMID 41348132,
  PMC12802582, doi 10.1158/2326-6066.cir-25-0514): manipulating antigen avidity
  changed persistence and efficacy together; early lymph-node trafficking was
  shown essential; efficacy survived loss of host T and B cells.

  Loss- and gain-of-function on persistence programs. Jain et al., Nature 2023
  (PMID 36755094, PMC10511001, doi 10.1038/s41586-022-05692-z) — TET2 disruption
  drives antigen-independent CAR T clonal expansion, requiring biallelic loss
  and sustained BATF3 via a MYC-dependent program; a dependency structure, not
  just a correlation, and it cuts against reading persistence as uniformly good.
  Kang et al., Science 2024 (PMID 39388542, PMC11697317, doi
  10.1126/science.adl4492) — Dnmt3a/Tet2/Asxl1-deficient CD8 cells preserve a
  progenitor-exhausted pool >1 year; note the human arm of that paper (survival
  advantage of mutated T cells in treated patients) is associative, not causal.
  Li et al., Front Immunol 2026 (PMID 42440569, PMC13333395) — NOSIP
  overexpression increases persistence and tumour control.

  Mechanistic but persistence only inferred. Turnquist et al., J Immunol 2026
  (PMID 42215073, PMC13221245) — PI3Kδ inhibition preserves stemness markers. Ao
  et al., Cancer Immunol Res 2026 (PMID 41719158, PMC13227094) — IL-15 lowers
  the TCR signalling threshold of TIL clonotypes in 3D models; no in vivo
  persistence measured. Zhao et al., Int J Biol Sci 2026 (PMID 41943833,
  PMC13050436) — CD39⁺CD8⁺ TIL reinfusion controls tumours in mice
  (interventional), while the patient-level clonality findings in the same paper
  are observational.

  Human quantitative dynamics, not adoptive therapy. Koftori et al., PLoS Biol
  2025 (PMID 40540506, PMC12251145, doi 10.1371/journal.pbio.3003179) used
  long-term in vivo labelling in volunteers to show CD95int and CD95hi TSCM are
  distinct populations with different clonal longevity. This is the most direct
  human evidence about which cells have staying power, but it is not a therapy
  study and tests no outcome.

  The gap, stated plainly

  I found no human study that experimentally manipulates clonotype persistence
  in adoptive T-cell therapy and measures the effect on outcome. Every human
  result above is correlative. That matters because of two specific problems the
  abstracts do not resolve:

  - Reverse causation is live throughout. Persisting clones need antigen. Tumour
    regression removes antigen, which can reduce persistence; tumour
    progression sustains antigen, which can sustain it. So "clones decayed and
    the patient progressed" (40341231) and "clones persisted and the patient
    responded" (41267983) are both compatible with persistence being a readout
    of tumour control rather than its cause.
  - Persistence is not intrinsically favourable. The TET2/BATF3 work (36755094)
    shows clonal persistence can be a proliferative, reduced-effector state
    prone to secondary mutations — so "more persistence" and "better therapy"
    are not the same variable.

  Two further caveats on the evidence base: many of the hits are 2026 papers
  with zero or low citation counts, so they are unreplicated; and several were
  single-patient or ≤6-patient reports where the abstract's causal language
  ("mechanistically linked," "drive") exceeds the design.

  If you want this firmed up, the useful next step is pulling full text on
  40341231, 42575986 and 31845905 — those three carry the most weight and are
  the ones where the statistical handling actually determines how much the
  associations are worth.
